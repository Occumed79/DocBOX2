# Provider Experience — Research Basis

This file is the research ledger for the public provider-onboarding experience.

## Editorial rules

- The finished experience tells the **company story**, not employee biographies.
- **Do not name clients** in the public-facing historical narrative. Public records can be used as corroborating evidence without surfacing the customer identity.
- Distinguish a **proprietary methodology** from a patented invention. Do not imply patent protection unless a patent is independently verified.
- Prefer external/public records for historical corroboration and Occu-Med's current site for present-day scale and current methodology language.
- Do not turn the history into a list of who joined the company and when. People should be named only when required to explain the origin of the company or methodology.
- When a current Occu-Med marketing statistic conflicts with a stronger independent benchmark, preserve the distinction in this ledger rather than silently presenting both as equivalent.

## Verified company-history beats

### 1976 — Research before the company

A 2018 public procurement response states that Occu-Med's founder directed a federally funded project in 1976 to develop and implement an improved system of physical and medical standards and procedures for employment. The source says the project eventually used more than $2 million in cash and in-kind support. It describes three goals: safe performance of essential job functions, reduction of worker-injury costs, and protection of disabled workers through a medically appropriate and legally defensible reasonable-accommodation methodology.

The same public record says the work later expanded through a broader California advisory effort and explicitly characterizes the later Occu-Med methodology as growing from this research.

Source: https://santaana.granicus.com/MetaViewer.php?clip_id=1900&meta_id=67982&view_id=2

### 1979 — Occu-Med founded in Honolulu

Occu-Med's current corporate history states that the company was founded in Honolulu in 1979 to help public and private employers reduce workforce health-and-safety costs.

A 2016 independent business profile separately reports that the company's original focus was research and consulting and that operations later expanded into occupational medical clinic management, workers' compensation cost containment, vocational rehabilitation counseling, and related services.

Sources:
- https://www.occu-med.com/who-we-are/
- https://thebusinessjournal.com/occu-med-globe-trotting-rising-star/

### Current methodology — three-part model

Occu-Med currently describes its approach as proprietary and research-based, integrating valid job demands / job information, job-related medical exams, and legal/job compatibility into one placement decision.

Sources:
- https://www.occu-med.com/who-we-are/
- https://www.occu-med.com/pre-placement-medical-evaluations/
- https://www.occu-med.com/what-we-do/

### 2006 — International markets

Occu-Med's current company history states that it has served international markets since 2006.

Source: https://www.occu-med.com/who-we-are/

### 2016 — International infrastructure and outside recognition

A contemporary 2016 profile reports that Occu-Med had developed infrastructure to perform pre-placement medical examinations in more than 36 countries. The same year, Fresno State's Institute for Family Business named Occu-Med the 2016 Rising Star Award winner.

The public-facing site can use the 36-country milestone and the existence of outside recognition, but should not surface customer names or client-specific case details from the underlying profile.

Sources:

Occu-Med currently describes a network of 15,000+ provider locations, coverage across all 50 states and U.S. territories, and 50+ countries abroad. Its current materials also state that more than one million employees have been supported.

## Independent workplace-injury context

Occu-Med's current site says that 41% of workplace injuries occur in the first year on the job. Independent Travelers data does **not** support that exact general-population percentage at present.

Travelers' 2025 Injury Impact Report analyzed 1.2 million workers' compensation claims from the preceding five years and found that first-year employees accounted for approximately **36% of injuries** and **34% of overall claim costs**. The 2024 Travelers report similarly reported 35% of all workers' compensation claims involving first-year employees.

For the public experience, use the independently supported **36% / 1.2M claims** benchmark rather than repeating the 41% figure as a general national statistic. If 41% is later corroborated for a specific population, dataset, or period, it can be restored with that scope stated explicitly.

Sources:
- https://investor.travelers.com/newsroom/press-releases/news-details/2025/Travelers-Releases-2025-Injury-Impact-Report/default.aspx
- https://investor.travelers.com/newsroom/press-releases/news-details/2024/Travelers-Publishes-2024-Injury-Impact-Report/default.aspx
- https://www.occu-med.com/who-we-are/

## Visual-story implications

1. **History should begin in 1976, not 1979.** The research question is the opening historical beat; the company is the result.
2. The history chapter should visualize the evolution **research → methodology → operating company → international system → global network**.
3. Historical records should be treated like archival evidence in the experience, inspired by the Nasdaq 50th-anniversary approach, rather than shown as ordinary citations or document screenshots.
4. The 1976 research record supports a dedicated archival scene built around the three research objectives and the scale of the project, without turning the scene into a founder biography.
5. The injury-risk scene should use independently verified workforce data and then transition directly into why job-specific context matters.
6. The proprietary-methodology section should make the three dimensions spatial and interactive rather than presenting three ordinary cards.
7. The global-network scene should function as both spectacle and information visualization, drawing conceptually from WWF Blue Corridors and Climate TRACE.
8. The immersive story should gradually settle into a much quieter, provider-specific onboarding interface.
9. The provider section should be functional, not decorative: specialty selection, capability selection, location handling, rate entry, and review should work before any submission backend is added.
