The really important discovery is that the Occu-Med story doesn't actually start in 1979. It starts earlier, with the research
that produced the methodology.
A strong archival source says that in 1976, Occu-Med's founder directed a project funded by the U.S. Civil Service
Commission to develop a better system of physical and medical standards for employment. The project reportedly
involved more than $2 million in cash and in-kind support and had three explicit goals: making sure workers could
safely perform essential job functions, reducing injury costs, and protecting disabled workers by creating a medically
appropriate and legally defensible way to determine reasonable accommodation. A secondary grant then expanded the
work across California and created an advisory group involving more than 30 counties plus other public agencies. The
source explicitly says that Occu-Med's methodology was born from that research.
That is enormously more important than "founded in Honolulu in 1979."
